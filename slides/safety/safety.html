B1 · Does Google see our children's videos?

Google only gets an anonymised copy

Gets

The video with faces blurred and names muted.

Never gets

The original, the transcript, any names.

How it's set up

  • EU processing only
  • Keeps nothing
  • Never trains on it
  • No Google employee reviews it
  • No tools, no internet search

Plan B: if the EU-US data agreement falls, we switch to an EU provider by configuration.

Model: Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite on Google Cloud's eu endpoint; global blocked by policy; zero data retention with the in-memory cache off; abuse-monitoring exception so no human review; listed as a sub-processor. The EU guarantee costs about 10% more, a fraction of a cent. Voices reach Google but aren't biometric data, because nothing identifies speakers. Schools that object can get a silent mode with no audio sent.

B5 · Accounts, leaked codes, deletion

We know teams, never students

Students

No accounts or emails. Class code plus a team word, like blue-falcon-42.

Leaked code

Useless without a team word. New teams need the teacher's OK.

Deletion request

Goes to the school. The teacher deletes it in one click.

Codes rotate with one click and expire when the project ends. Staff log in with email and password; admins also need a second factor. The school is the data controller, FrameUp the processor, and we give schools a ready-made impact assessment.
B6 · Is this legal under the AI Act?

Likely "high-risk", and designed for it

Already today

  • No emotion recognition
  • Every feedback labelled as AI
  • Training for teachers and creators

By December 2027

  • A human can always step in
  • Every step is logged
  • Every change is tested first

No grades, no consequences: the feedback never feeds into marks.

AI that evaluates learning outcomes is listed as high-risk (Annex III 3(b)). The AI Omnibus (Regulation 2026/1744) moved those rules to 2 December 2027. Free tools aren't exempt. Still to do before then: quality management, an internal conformity assessment, EU database registration. Because there are no grades and a human can always change the feedback, GDPR Art. 22 on automated decisions shouldn't apply. Not legal advice: FrameUp confirms with a data protection officer in the pilot.
B7 · Running our own models safely

One locked server, no AI experts needed

Why

The transcript and the feedback text never leave FrameUp. No cost per feedback.

The rules

  • Never reachable from the internet
  • Only our app can talk to it
  • Fixed model version, tested first

The trade-off: a fixed monthly server bill.

Ollama has no login of its own, and over ten thousand Ollama servers were found openly reachable in 2026, hence rule one. Access goes through an authenticated proxy, with no outgoing internet except planned updates. Recommended models: Qwen3 or Mistral Small (Apache 2.0).
B8 · Quick answers

Edge cases

A student mentions self-harm

No AI reply. The teacher is alerted at once.

History video with war footage

Goes to the teacher, never blocked silently.

The AI is mean or wrong

Caught by the tone check. Facts are only "please double-check".

Blurring misses a face

It blurs too much rather than too little. Google keeps nothing.

Training on the videos?

Never by default. Only with separate consent.

Too many false alarms?

Each costs a minute. We tune in the pilot.
